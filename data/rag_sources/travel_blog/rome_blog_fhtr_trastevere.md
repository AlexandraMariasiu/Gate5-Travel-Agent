# A day in Trastevere: what to do, where to eat, and how not to waste it

Here is something that comes up regularly in conversations with our guests: **Trastevere is on their list of must sees**, but somehow it keeps getting treated as a separate expedition requiring advance planning: a commitment we would expect from a hiking trip, rather than from a neighborhood visit.

At From Home to Home, we understand some of the rationale behind such an approach. Maps can be misleading, and **Rome’s geography has a way of making things that are very close feel very far.** But Trastevere is, quite literally, on the other side of the river Tiber, which in Rome means a bridge away, not a continent away. And here’s something else: we do manage some apartments in Trastevere proper. Indeed, you could be staying around the corner from the main sights of this fashionable *rione*, if you wanted to!

## **Getting to Trastevere: simpler than you think**

**The most pleasant way to reach Trastevere from the historic center is simply to walk** across one of the Tiber’s central bridges — Ponte Sisto, Ponte Garibaldi, or Ponte Mazzini all deposit you either directly into the neighborhood or a few minutes from its edge.

If you’re coming from further afield, **tram 8 connects Largo Argentina to Trastevere in about ten minutes and runs frequently throughout the day. Bus H connects Termini to Viale di Trastevere**.

There is no metro stop in Trastevere, which is arguably one of the reasons it has retained more of its character than other central neighborhoods — the slight friction of getting there keeps a certain category of visitors away.

## **What to do once you’re in Trastevere?**

**Start at the Mercato di San Cosimato**

If you’re arriving in the morning — and for this neighborhood, morning is the right time to arrive — begin at the covered market in Piazza di San Cosimato. It’s a working neighborhood market, not a tourist market: the vendors sell to locals who live here, which means the produce is good, the prices are honest, and nobody is going to try to sell you a branded tote bag. Pick up something to eat later, talk to people, take your time. This is the Trastevere that exists before the TikTok crowd arrives (which is, you guessed it, later in the day).

**Have a coffee at Bar San Calisto**

Bar San Calisto in Piazza di San Calisto is one of those places that has remained stubbornly, irreducibly itself despite being surrounded by a neighborhood that changes a little more each year. It opens early, it stays open late, and in between its opening and closing times it caters to a clientele that is incredibly varied: from teenagers to older neighborhood gentlemen, everyone will stop at “Sancalisto” at least once if they’re nearby!

**Visit Santa Maria in Trastevere**

One of the oldest churches in Rome, and one of its most beautiful. The twelfth-century mosaics on the façade and inside the apse are extraordinary by any standard. Entry is free, as with all churches in Rome.

**The Galleria Corsini: a world-class museum without the queues**

Housed in the eighteenth-century Palazzo Corsini on Via della Lungara, the Galleria Nazionale d’Arte Antica di Palazzo Corsini is the kind of museum that shouldn’t be as quiet as it is. Its collection includes works by Caravaggio, Rubens, Van Dyck, and Guido Reni, displayed in rooms that feel more like a private residence than a public institution (spoiler alert: it’s because, until relatively recently, they were!). No timed entry, no queuing system, no audio guide with a queue of its own. You simply walk in, pay a modest entrance fee, and have one of the finest collections of Baroque painting in Italy almost entirely to yourself. It is, frankly, one of Rome’s best-kept secrets. And if you feel that way inclined, you can also visit its older brother, for free…

**Walk up to the Gianicolo**

From Trastevere, the Gianicolo hill is a fifteen-minute walk uphill — worth every step for the panorama that opens up at the top. The view takes in almost the entire city, from the Vittoriano to St. Peter’s dome, and is particularly good in the late afternoon when the light shifts. At noon, a cannon is fired from the hill — a tradition dating back to 1847 — which will either delight you or cause you to spill your coffee, depending on how much warning you’ve had.

**Browse the Porta Portese flea market (Sundays only)**

If your visit falls on a Sunday morning, Porta Portese is non-negotiable. One of the largest flea markets in Europe, it stretches for kilometers along the streets behind Viale di Trastevere and sells everything from antique furniture to vintage clothing to things that defy categorization. Go early — by 10am it’s busy, by noon it’s packed, and by 2pm the vendors are packing up. While most vendors are getting card readers, it’s best to bring some cash here. Bargaining not always welcome, so choose wisely where to try it.

**More shopping? We’ve got you covered!**

From bookstores to vinyl shops, from vintage clothing to sensational independent fashion brands, this district has a bit of everything for everyone. Unless you’re on the main drag, Viale di Trastevere, expect very small spaces (these are Medieval buildings, after all!). Look for some of our favorites, from Elvis Lives for original souvenirs to Antica Caciara Trasteverina for food, from Omar Roma for glass-made items to the aptly named Trastevere Store if you’re looking to support a different football team, all these alleys are a true treasure trove.

**Aperitivo at a Trastevere enoteca**

The neighborhood has no shortage of places to drink, ranging from the excellent to the tourist-facing and overpriced. The ones worth your time are generally the smaller enoteche — wine bars with a short list, simple snacks, and enough space to have an actual conversation. Avoid anywhere with a laminated menu propped in the window and a chalk sign advertising a “happy hour special.” Trust the places that look like nobody tried very hard to make them look inviting.

**Dinner: see above!**

“Trust the places that look like nobody tried very hard to make them look inviting” is very much solid advice for choosing a restaurant in the neighborhood. If you need more tips, follow ours on how to avoid tourist traps: Trastevere has seen an exponential rise in tourist numbers, and unscrupolous restaurateurs are very much a thing here as well. Most of all, don’t fall for queues: social media influencers have created some real monsters there, but there are perfectly good alternatives where all you need to enjoy a good meal is a… reservation.

**A note on timing in Trastevere**

Trastevere after 9pm on a Friday or Saturday is a different place from the Trastevere we have been describing. The narrow streets fill with groups moving between bars, the noise levels climb, and the neighborhood takes on an atmosphere that has more to do with a university town on a big night out than with the Rome you came to find. This is not a criticism — it is simply a fact worth knowing so you can plan accordingly. **If a lively evening is what you’re after, it delivers. If you came for something quieter, go on a weekday, or go early and leave before aperitivo time!**
